# Tessaly Atoll Reef Survey 2024

## Summary

Between April and June 2024, a team of researchers and 140 volunteer divers surveyed 63 sites around Tessaly Atoll. Average live coral cover was 34 percent, down from 48 percent recorded in the 2015 survey. The decline is attributed mainly to the mass bleaching event of February 2024, when sea surface temperature peaked at 31.2 degrees Celsius, nearly two degrees above the long-term summer maximum.

## Methods

At each site, divers laid a 50-metre transect along the reef crest at a depth of 8 to 10 metres and photographed one square metre quadrats every 5 metres. Photographs were scored for coral cover using point-count software, with 50 random points per image. Fish were counted within 2.5 metres on either side of the transect. Water temperature was logged hourly by loggers fixed at 12 of the sites.

## Coral findings

Massive Porites corals were the most resilient group, losing only 6 percent of cover, while branching Acropora lost more than half. The north-facing sites, shaded by the island in the afternoon, kept 41 percent cover compared with 27 percent on the exposed south side. Recovery of juvenile corals was recorded at 19 of the 63 sites, mostly in the sheltered lagoon.

## Fish and fishing

Herbivorous fish, which keep algae in check, were more abundant than in 2015. Parrotfish biomass rose by 22 percent after the district banned net fishing on the outer reef in 2020. Large predatory fish such as groupers remain scarce, with an average of 0.4 individuals per transect.

## Recommendations

The authors recommend extending the ban on net fishing to the lagoon, installing mooring buoys to create no-anchor zones at the 10 most visited sites, and repeating the survey every two years instead of every nine. They also recommend that the marine park fund a permanent monitoring station on the north shore.
