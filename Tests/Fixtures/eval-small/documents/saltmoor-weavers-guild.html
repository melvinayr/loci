The Saltmoor Weavers' Guild

Foundation

The guild was founded in 1342 by 31 weavers of the market town of Saltmoor, who petitioned the lord of the manor for the exclusive right to sell woollen cloth within the borough. The charter, written in Latin on a single sheet of vellum, set the annual membership fee at four pence.

Apprentices and masters

Boys were admitted as apprentices at the age of twelve, on payment of six shillings and a promise of obedience from their parents. Apprenticeship lasted seven years. At the end, the apprentice presented a sample cloth to the wardens, and if it passed inspection he became a journeyman.

The number of master weavers was limited to twenty-four, so that the town's trade would not be divided among too many workshops. A journeyman could become a master only when a place fell vacant and he could pay an entry fee of forty shillings.

Standards and fines

Cloth sold in Saltmoor had to be at least two ells wide, about 90 inches, and was marked with the guild seal after inspection. A weaver caught selling undersized cloth was fined three shillings and four pence for a first offence, and lost the right to trade for a year after a third. Wardens inspected the looms of members four times a year.

Widows and women

Though women could not become masters in their own right, the widow of a master was permitted to run his workshop for as long as she remained unmarried, and records show that 17 widows did so between 1380 and 1500.

The guildhall

The guildhall in Bridge Street was built in 1368. It burned down in the great fire of 1471, destroying most of the early ledgers. The rebuilt hall, completed in 1476, housed the guild's wool store on the ground floor and its meeting room above.
