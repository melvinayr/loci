A Practical Guide to Sourdough Bread

Keeping a starter

A sourdough starter is a living culture of wild yeast and lactic acid bacteria kept in a mixture of flour and water. To keep it healthy, feed it at a ratio of 1:1:1 by weight: one part starter, one part flour and one part water. A jar kept on the counter should be fed every day; a jar kept in the refrigerator needs feeding only once a week, and should be brought to room temperature and fed twice before baking.

A hungry starter smells sharp, like nail polish remover or acetone. A starter that has just been fed smells mild and yeasty. If a dark liquid called hooch forms on top, the starter is very hungry; stir it back in or pour it off and feed. Pink or orange streaks mean the starter has spoiled and must be thrown away.

Making the dough

The standard loaf in this guide uses 500 grams of bread flour, 375 grams of water, 100 grams of active starter and 10 grams of salt. The recipe has a hydration of 75 percent, since the water weighs 75 percent as much as the flour. Mix the flour and water first and rest for 45 minutes, a step bakers call the autolyse, then add the starter and salt.

Bulk fermentation takes four to five hours at 24 degrees Celsius. During the first two hours, fold the dough every 30 minutes. The dough is ready when it has grown by about half, feels airy and shows bubbles along the edges. In a cold kitchen, the same stage can take up to nine hours, so judge by the dough and not the clock.

Shaping and baking

Shape the dough into a tight round or oval and place it seam side up in a floured banneton. Proof it in the refrigerator for 12 to 16 hours; the long cold rest develops flavour and makes the dough easier to score.

Heat a Dutch oven in the oven at 245 degrees Celsius for 45 minutes. Bake the loaf with the lid on for 20 minutes, which traps steam and lets the loaf rise fully, then remove the lid and bake for another 25 minutes until the crust is deep brown. Cool on a rack for at least one hour before cutting.

Troubleshooting

A dense, gummy crumb with small holes usually means the dough was under-proofed. A loaf that spreads sideways and bakes flat was probably over-proofed or too wet. A pale crust means the oven was not hot enough or the lid came off too early. Large irregular holes just under a thick top crust point to too little steam.
